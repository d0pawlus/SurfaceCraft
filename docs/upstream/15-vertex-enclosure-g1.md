# Request: exact G1 across a network of patches at vertices of any valence (draft)

Status: draft, not filed. Checked against solidcore head `f02d13e9ce38f45a8cd4b5a794fa09ae81d2221e` (2026-11-15). Searched the head for: a network-level G1 construction, "vertex enclosure", Gregory or twist-compatible patches around a vertex, a constrained fit of cross-boundary derivatives of a patch network. Found only pieces (kernel paths): `crates/solidcore/src/nurbs/srf_match.rs` (one boundary of one surface matched to one other surface at G0/G1/G2, at stations, and "between stations it holds where the target is the same all along the boundary"; no vertex logic); `crates/solidcore/src/nurbs/srf_adv/p4.rs` and `b_spline_surface_n_lib/p1.rs` (bicubic Coons with cross-boundary derivatives; the corner twist compatibility of ONE patch is checked there and the twists averaged); `crates/solidcore/src/nurbs/n_sided.rs` (one n-sided hole as N patches round a centre; G0 certified, "tangency across the inner curves (G1) is the fill's aim ... not certified here"; the loop's outer neighbours are not constrained); `crates/solidcore/src/nurbs/gordon.rs`; `crates/solidcore-ops/src/stitch.rs` (sewing). No network-level construction. Duplicates (`gh issue list --repo tangency-labs/solidcraft --state all`, read only; searched "vertex enclosure", "extraordinary vertex", "G1 across vertices", "tangent continuous network", "Bezier patch network G1"): no issue asks for it. Related, open: tangency-labs/solidcraft#14374 (a multi-patch network fitted with seams that are G1 where requested: its requirement is this operation, its "required capabilities" name a "constrained network fitting capability"), #759, #11672 (a patching algorithm like XNURBS), #772. #763 (boundary surface, G0/G1/G2 per edge) is closed.
Pin: `solidcore` rev `4f2da85c26f7d608808d94cffcf06ebe867ef61d`.

Motivation, one line: patch networks laid over a smooth shape need tangent-plane continuity across every edge, including at vertices where more or fewer than four patches meet, and no kernel entry builds that.

## The problem

A network of bicubic (or higher degree) patches shares its boundary curves; every edge between two patches is to be G1 (one tangent plane along it, within a tolerance the caller states), except edges the caller marks as creases. At a vertex of valence 4 the two cross-boundary derivatives of a patch, one per side, can be chosen independently up to the corner twist compatibility that `srf_adv/p4.rs` already checks for one patch. At any other valence the cross-boundary derivatives of the patches around the vertex constrain each other (the vertex-enclosure constraint of the patch-network literature): picking each edge's cross-derivative from its two end tangents independently, as is usual, does not give exact G1 along every edge at such a vertex. Exactness needs the derivatives around the vertex chosen jointly, which is a small linear problem per vertex, and the edges' own derivative fields chosen to meet it.

## The operation asked for

Input: a list of patches (control nets, degree given), each with its four sides, an adjacency list (patch, side, patch, side, orientation), and per edge a flag `smooth` or `crease`. Optional: per patch soft position targets (points and weights) the free control points should follow, and the degree the caller allows (raising it is acceptable if the construction needs it).

Output: the patches with their control points set so that, within the stated angle tolerance, the tangent planes of the two patches agree along every smooth edge, at every vertex whatever its valence; the shared boundary curves unchanged (the network stays watertight); crease edges left as G0.

Report, not asserted: per smooth edge the largest angle between the two patch normals on a dense sample (not only at stations), and per vertex whether the joint conditions had a solution; a vertex with none is named (a fold, an inverted corner) and the operation refuses, or leaves that vertex's patches as the caller's input and says so, as the caller chooses. This is the measure `solidcore::query::continuity` already gives per face pair.

Not asked: choosing the layout, fitting the boundary curves to data, G2, trimming.

Notes for the owner (not part of the text to post):

1. What the application has, from the code. Two constructions, both in `crates/fit/src`:
   - `patch.rs` (one bicubic Bézier patch per quad of an unstructured layout; listed in `xtask/app-geometry.txt`, with `features.rs`): neighbours share a cubic Bézier edge and have the same tangent plane at every layout vertex (the plane of the vertex normal). Along an edge the cross-boundary rows are interpolated from the two end tangents and flattened into one plane per edge; the two predictions of an inner control point are averaged. The module doc says the planes agree exactly at vertices and approximately along edges, "measured, not claimed zero", and that exact G1 at vertices of any valence "needs the vertex-enclosure construction".
   - `patch_g1.rs` (`surface.wrap` with `continuity: g1`; each coarse triangle becomes three patches): the normal field `n(t)` of an edge is the end normals blended; both neighbours are asked for a cross-boundary derivative perpendicular to it. The condition is linear, so each patch solves its four inner control points by weighted least squares from scan samples under it and the conditions on its smooth sides (5 samples per side, weights `G1_SIGMAS` 0.004 then 0.04). The conditions are exact at vertices, least-squares along edges, "at vertices of any valence: no vertex-enclosure compatibility is needed because the neighbours do not have to agree on a specific cross derivative, only on its plane".
2. Where it falls short, from the code: along an edge G1 holds in the least-squares sense only; `jump_stats` (`patch_jumps.rs`) and `edge_normal_jumps` measure it. A patch whose tangent net folds (`follows`: normal facing the coarse face below 0.3 cosine), has an undefined normal, or is worse than 1.25 times the unconstrained patch's scan rms plus 0.02 mm, is replaced by the unconstrained patch (`patch_net` returns `(free, false)`; counted in `G1Wrap::fallbacks`). Measured by the application on synthetic data (docs/STATUS.md, gate `gate_p4_closed_organic_blob`: a sphere with bulges, 276 patches): normal jump across edges max 3.1 degrees, mean 0.30 degrees. STATUS.md also records, for `continuity: g1` on harder scans, folded patches at badly shaped layout triangles (up to 96 fallbacks of 510 patches). Those are the application's numbers, not kernel ones.
3. NOT FOUND in the code: an "even valence condition". Neither `patch.rs` nor `patch_g1.rs` mentions parity of valence; the only valence statements are the two quoted above (any valence). The brief for this draft named such a condition; the draft therefore does not state one. If you know of it from the literature, add it with its source before posting; I did not want to claim a construction detail I could not read in the code.
4. The text avoids naming a degree or a specific construction on purpose: which construction (a degree raise, Gregory-type patches, a constrained fit of the edge ribbons) is the maintainers' choice. The application's request is the operation and its report.
5. If the kernel's `n_sided` fill is accepted by the maintainers as covering part of this (a single hole), the request still stands for the outer neighbours' continuity and for networks.
